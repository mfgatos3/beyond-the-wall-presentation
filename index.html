Slide 02

Problem Statement & Context

Social friction in vulnerable neighborhoods

Communities in Ibagué face fragmentation — lack of safe youth spaces, limited mental health support, unresolved social tensions between neighbors.

Stigmatization of urban art

Graffiti is dismissed as vandalism, erasing its role as collective memory, cultural identity, and a form of social resistance in marginalized communities.

Conflict on neighborhood courts

Local sports fields become sites of aggression. Unregulated competition fuels disputes rather than building cooperation or leadership skills.

Emotional & mental health gaps

Youth lack accessible tools for emotional regulation, stress management, and constructive conflict resolution in their daily environments.

"Unifying urban art, tactical strategy, and emotional self-regulation transforms neighborhood courts into spaces of youth leadership."

Slide 03

Justification & Interdisciplinary Integration

Why integrate five subjects?

Art, sport, and emotions don't happen in isolation in young people's lives. A holistic approach mirrors real-world complexity and produces deeper learning.

CLIL methodology

Content and Language Integrated Learning — students develop English proficiency while engaging with authentic content across all five disciplines.

Interactive simulators

Digital tools let learners practice decision-making under pressure — tactical plays, emotional regulation, community conflict — in a safe environment.

Service-learning bridge

The project connects academic knowledge with real community impact: fair-play tournaments, collaborative murals, and restorative dialogue circles.

Slide 04

Project Objectives

General Objective

Build an interactive web platform integrating urban art analysis, tactical sports strategy, emotional self-regulation, and servant leadership to empower youth as agents of community transformation.

Specific Objectives

  1. Analyze urban art as a vehicle for collective memory and identity, decoding murals through semiotic and socio-historical lenses.
  2. Evaluate tactical sports decisions under competitive pressure using interactive video breakdowns and spatial diagrams.
  3. Develop a digital self-regulation simulator for practicing box breathing, grounding, and conflict de-escalation during match scenarios.
  4. Design a servant leadership playbook to organize fair-play tournaments, community murals, and restorative dialogue circles.
Slide 05

Theoretical Framework

Key authors grounding each module.

Visual Culture & Semiotics

Baudrillard (1993) · Awad (2020)

Urban art as symbolic space of resistance — graffiti as semiotic practice reclaiming public narrative and encoding collective identity.

Tactical Performance

Garganta (2009) · Holt et al. (2017)

Decision-making under competitive stress — spatial awareness, temporal pressure, and group dynamics shaping tactical intelligence.

Emotion Regulation

Gross (2015) · Smith et al. (2021)

Process model of emotion regulation and digital simulation — cognitive reappraisal and grounding techniques practiced interactively.

Servant Leadership & CLIL

Greenleaf (2002) · Coyle (2010)

Leadership rooted in human dignity and community service, combined with CLIL for bilingual competence through authentic engagement.

Platform Modules

Four interconnected modules powering the experience.

The Urban Canvas

Interactive gallery with socio-historical critique of street art, stencils, and community murals. Users decode symbolism through guided visual analysis.

Video analysis and spatial play diagrams for real match scenarios. Users evaluate decisions, identify patterns, and explore field simulations.

Physical Education · English
03

Self-Regulation & Match Simulator

Glossary of regulation techniques (box breathing, grounding, reappraisal) plus a conflict simulator in match contexts.

Socio-Emotional Development · English
04

Servant Leadership Playbook

Action guide for organizing fair-play tournaments, collaborative murals, and restorative dialogue circles in the community.
